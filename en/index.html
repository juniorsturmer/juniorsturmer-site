FAQ

Frequently asked questions

My company already sells well. Why invest in brand strategy?

Because the brand is what sustains growth when referrals and the owner's effort are no longer enough. A clear brand helps the right client find you, understand you and choose you with less effort from sales. According to McKinsey, B2B companies with strong brands outperform those with weak brands by 20%.

Source: McKinsey, B2B business branding
What's the difference between a logo and a brand?

The logo is the signature. The brand is what people think and feel when they remember your company: the promise, the experience and the reputation. A beautiful logo without strategy behind it doesn't tell clients why they should choose you. That's why my work starts with the brand and ends with the visuals.

How does a brand affect the price I can charge?

Brands perceived as different compete less on price. Kantar research shows customers are willing to pay more, in some cases up to twice as much, for brands they see as meaningfully different. Warren Buffett put it simply: the single most important decision in evaluating a business is pricing power.

Source: Kantar via MediaPost, Blueprint for Brand Growth
My company is B2B. Does brand matter when selling to other businesses?

A lot. A study by the Ehrenberg-Bass Institute for the LinkedIn B2B Institute shows that up to 95% of potential buyers are not ready to buy at any given time. When the need comes up, they call whoever they already remember. Your brand is what makes that memory yours.

Source: Marketing Week, Ehrenberg-Bass and LinkedIn B2B Institute
What is brand positioning and why does it matter?

It's the place your company holds in the client's mind and the reason it gets chosen over competitors. Without positioning, everyone on the team sells differently and communication changes with every campaign. According to BCG, companies with strong brands have a 46% larger market share than those with weaker brands.

Source: BCG, Why brand marketing matters
How do I know it's time to reposition or refresh my brand?

Some signs: the company has grown but the brand still tells the old story, clients compare you to cheaper competitors, or every piece of material looks different. Consistency also shows up in revenue: a Lucidpress and Demand Metric study, cited by Inc., estimates a 23% average revenue increase when a brand is presented consistently.

Source: Inc., brand consistency
